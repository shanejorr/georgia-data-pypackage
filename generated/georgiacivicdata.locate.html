georgiacivicdata.locate#

georgiacivicdata.locate(lat=None, lon=None, address=None, id=None, grade=None, year=None, *, quiet=False, backend=None)#

Locate points or addresses.

The county, 2020 Census tract and block group, school district and zoned schools that points or street addresses fall in.

Points are sent to the API's POST /locate route in batches of up to 1,000, so coordinates never appear in URLs and nothing is cached. Addresses never reach the Georgia Civic Data API: the package geocodes them on your computer with the U.S. Census batch geocoder (https://geocoding.geo.census.gov/geocoder/, benchmark Public_AR_Current, the address data the API's own search uses; up to 10,000 addresses per upload), then sends the coordinates as points. An address the Census can't match is never sent. To geocode with another service, geocode first and pass lat and lon.

A zone is the default assignment, not the only option: a student may attend another public school by transfer, or a charter, magnet or other choice school. This is not an official enrollment determination; confirm with the school district.

Output. A long data frame: for each input, one row per place it falls in. A typical location gives seven rows: its county, tract, block group and school district, then one row per zoned school. Rows for each input come in a fixed order: county, tract, block group, district, then elementary, middle and high schools by grade.

A level can have more than one school row: some homes are zoned to one elementary school for kindergarten to grade 2 and another for grades 3-5. The grades column tells them apart; pass grade to keep only the school for one grade.

The note column is missing normally, and one plain sentence otherwise, so a missing row is never silent:

  • a level with no zone collected yet, a gap between zones, or a level served by another district gets a row with missing id, name and grades, and a note saying why;

  • an input outside Georgia, a point the API rejects, or an address the Census can't match gets one row with missing geographic columns and the reason; one bad input never stops the batch;

  • in a Department of Defense district the district row has a missing id and a note;

  • school rows whose zone has a known pending change, or which overlap another school's zone, say so.

Parameters:

  • lat (float or sequence of float, optional) -- Latitude and longitude in decimal degrees (WGS 84), of equal length.

  • lon (float or sequence of float, optional) -- Latitude and longitude in decimal degrees (WGS 84), of equal length.

  • address (str, sequence of str, or DataFrame, optional) -- Street addresses: one-line addresses ("206 Washington St SW, Atlanta, GA 30334"), or a data frame (pandas or Polars) or dict with columns street, city, state and zip. Give either lat and lon or address, not both.

  • id (sequence, optional) -- Identifiers for the inputs, the same length, carried into the output so results join back to your data. Default: each input's position, starting at 0.

  • grade (str or int, optional) -- Keep only school zones serving this grade ("pk", "k", "01" to "12"; "5", 5 and "K" are accepted). Default: every grade.

  • year (int, optional) -- The zones' school year as its ending year (2027 is 2026-27). Default: the current zones.

  • quiet (bool, default False) -- True silences the once-per-session note on the default assignment.

  • backend ({"pandas", "polars"}, optional) -- The kind of data frame to return. Default: the backend option.

Returns:

pandas.DataFrame or polars.DataFrame -- Columns, in order:

  1. id.

  2. The input: lat, lon for points; for addresses address, the geocoder's match_status ("match", "no_match", "tie"), match_type ("exact", "non_exact"), matched_address, and the geocoded lat and lon.

  3. geographic_type: "county", "tract", "block_group", "school_district", "elementary_school", "middle_school" or "high_school".

  4. geographic_id: county FIPS ("13121"), tract GEOID ("13121003500"), block group GEOID ("131210035001"), district code ("761"), or for a school "DISTRICT-SCHOOL" ("761-4560"), the form rows() takes as school_id.

  5. geographic_name: the place's name.

  6. grades: a school row's grade range ("K-5", "6-8", "9-12"); missing on other rows.

  7. note: missing, or why a row is missing or needs care.

meta() on the result gives the API's assignment_note and note on reading the zones.

See also

school_zones

the zones' shapes and precision (method, accuracy_class).

rows

with school_id, data on a located school.

Examples

>>> import georgiacivicdata as gcd

The Georgia State Capitol, by coordinates and by address:

>>> gcd.locate(lat=33.7490, lon=-84.3880)
>>> gcd.locate(address="206 Washington St SW, Atlanta, GA 30334")

Only the school for one grade:

>>> gcd.locate(lat=33.7490, lon=-84.3880, grade=9)
